          How “For you” decides

          Each posting that can be read is searched for the sentences that say who may apply: year of study, graduation date, enrolment, returning to study, co-op registration, dates and length. Those sentences are compared with your facts. A published match means the posting names your situation and nothing in it conflicts. Conditional means it turns on something not settled, usually your master's place. Unknown means the posting says nothing relevant, or has not been read. A mismatch always shows the sentence it rests on. How well a posting was read is shown separately: in full from the employer's own system, partly, or not at all.

          Which roles count as one

          A vacancy is identified by its requisition on the employer's system, so the same title in London and New York stays two roles, each with its own link. Lists that link through their own site are attached to an employer posting only when employer, title, type and town all agree.

          The pay band

          Roles are tagged against a graduate pay band: £60,000 a year in the UK, about $95,000 in the US and C$100,000 in Canada. A solid tag means this posting states pay at or above the band in that country's currency. An outlined tag means published reports put the firm's graduate pay there, which is a statement about the firm and not this role. A dashed tag is an estimate from the kind of firm. Intern pay is pro rata.

          How pay is handled

          Stated pay is shown in the currency the employer gave. For sorting and the salary filter it is converted to US dollars at a rough fixed rate. Where a US role states none, a dashed figure shows an estimate: the median of the same employer's stated pay for the same role type. Hourly pay is converted to a yearly figure at 2,080 hours. The green edge marks pay above the national median for full-time workers: $64,064 in the US (BLS, Q3 2026), £40,911 in the UK (ONS, April 2026) and C$68,640 in Canada (Statistics Canada, 2025).

          Dates

          “First listed” is the day a role first appeared here, which is what “new” means on this site. Posting dates come from the source and many roles have none. Closing dates are read from the posting when it states one.
